VAN1, run by Cologix: operating. Inside a shared building (floors or a suite in a building with other occupants). Facts as the sources state them; missing ones are “not published”.

Beyond Data is a thesis research tool. Its plans, heat figures and costs are feasibility estimates built from public data and stated assumptions. They are not construction drawings, engineering designs, cost quotes or approvals, and no site owner, building or operator shown here has agreed to anything. Code and zoning checks screen each plan against the rules listed on its board; they are not a code review. Items marked ‘resolved in design development’ are open by design.

Sources

Facilities: the tool’s data (register resident_facilities): the data-centre inventory, research 2–3 Nov 2026 with check 2, addresses and published floors 12 Nov 2026. Each cell keeps its label: OC operator claim, GOV government record, BRK broker claim, ENG engineer’s project sheet, OBS site visit (author), DIR directory listing, ? not confirmed.

Placed: Vancouver by address in the City’s records (the parcel the tool’s own address search finds first: our method); Edmonton by the City permit geocode (arch_inv_edmPermitRows). No coordinates are published for the Vancouver rows.

3-D massing: City of Vancouver building footprints 2009 with City LiDAR 2022 heights (90th percentile of roof points), and buildings missing from them added from Public Safety Canada, Canada Structures 2024 (heights from the same LiDAR where it saw them). Floors drawn only where a source names them and gives the storey count, at height × floor ÷ storeys (our reading; decision of the author, 11 Nov 2026).

Drawings: the Engineering 3-D kit (generic units at their real scale class), rendered one at a time; not this facility’s equipment. Parts: named only where the facility’s sources name them (types, not counts).

Noise: the same rule as the Municipal card “Noise limits around the lot” (Vancouver Noise Control By-law 6555 ss. 2.3, 4.1(c); Edmonton Bylaw 14600); the limit at each lot, not a measurement.

Heat given off: the servers’ power over a year, size × 8,760 h × the average load 0.4–0.6 (the same load as the Engineering cards). The Engineering card “Heat offered” is larger: it adds the heat pumps’ own electricity.

Approval and notices: the Municipal card “Approval process” (Zoning and Development By-law s. 4; Edmonton Zoning Bylaw 20001 s. 7.160).

Confidence, 0–100, under each figure: the lowest of its inputs. 100 arithmetic · 90 regulation read directly · 80 government data · 70 research, other context · 60 derived, stated assumption · 50 operator or industry claim · 40 proxy · 20 judgement · 0 gap. A figure with no tag is not yet linked to its sources in the tool’s register.
