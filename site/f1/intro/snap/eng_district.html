How demand is estimated

Each building: footprint area × storeys (measured height ÷ 3.5 m; one storey where no height is measured) × the delivered heat a building of that use needs a year × 0.80–0.90 (the gas boiler a loop replaces). Use comes from the parcel’s zone (residential, commercial, industrial; CD-1 and Direct Control unknown) until assessment-use data is in. Intensities: NRCan CEUD 2023, BC (offices 125, retail 147, schools 137, health 306, hotels 242, recreation 181, all commercial 159 kWh/m²); residential: the NEU reference building, 109 kWh/m² (a new building; older stock uses more). Industrial: counted as transportation and warehousing (150 kWh/m², a proxy). CD-1, Direct Control and unknown uses: no figure, not counted.

Heating type

Only buildings on a district network are marked hydronic. No building-by-building heating data is in the tool yet, so every other building reads “unknown”: the tool never assumes a building can connect. City of Vancouver NEU rule (Connectivity Guidelines May 2026, 6.3): a receiver needs hydronic heating and hot water that can return at 45 °C or below; forced-air or baseboard buildings need a retrofit.

The share of the peak

A district must meet the coldest day. The data centre runs all year at about its offered heat ÷ 8,760 h; the cluster’s peak is its annual heat ÷ a district network’s full-load hours, 2,576–2,681 h (FVB 2023, Mississauga; diversity included). A base source sized to 30 % of peak carries about 90 % of the annual heat, 45 % about 99 % (Hiscock, CIBSE Journal 2023, UK modelling). Below 30 % the table gives nothing: shown as a placeholder. The rest comes from a gas (or electric) peaking boiler.
